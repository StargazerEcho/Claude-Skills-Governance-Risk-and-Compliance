Building a TPRM program from scratch for ~200 vendors is very manageable if you start with tiering rather than trying to apply uniform diligence to everyone. Here's a practical design.

Step 1: Inventory and initial triage
Before tiering, get a basic inventory: vendor name, business owner, what service they provide, what data they touch (none / internal / confidential / regulated PII / payment data), whether they connect to your systems (API, network, SSO/OAuth), and annual spend. Even a rough spreadsheet is enough to start - you can refine as the program matures.

Step 2: Tiering model
A three- or four-tier model balances rigor with practicality for 200 vendors. I'd recommend four tiers based on two combined factors: (a) data sensitivity/access and (b) business criticality (would an outage or breach materially disrupt operations, cause regulatory exposure, or harm customers).

Tier 1 - Critical: Vendors with access to regulated data (PII, PHI, financial/payment data), production system access, or that are single points of failure for a core business process (e.g., payroll, core banking platform, cloud infrastructure, identity provider). Typically 5-10% of vendors.
Tier 2 - High: Vendors with access to confidential but non-regulated data, or that support important but not mission-critical functions (e.g., marketing automation with customer contact data, HR systems, some SaaS with broad integration access). Typically 15-20%.
Tier 3 - Moderate: Vendors with limited data access or narrow system integration, moderate business impact (e.g., a scheduling tool, a design tool with no sensitive data). Typically 30-40%.
Tier 4 - Low: Vendors with no data access, no system integration, low spend, easily replaceable (e.g., office supplies, one-off consulting with no data sharing). Typically remaining 30-40%.

Step 3: Due diligence by tier

Tier 1 (Critical):
- Full security questionnaire (SIG, CAIQ, or custom) covering security, privacy, BCP/DR, subprocessors.
- Require SOC 2 Type II (or ISO 27001) report, reviewed annually, plus bridge letters between reports.
- Review of subprocessor/fourth-party list.
- Contractual: DPA, security addendum, breach notification SLA (e.g., 24-72 hours), right-to-audit clause, cyber insurance minimums, termination/data-return/destruction terms.
- Annual reassessment, plus ad hoc review after any material incident or M&A event involving the vendor.
- Executive/business owner sign-off before onboarding.
- Consider a pre-contract risk assessment call or on-site/virtual audit for the highest-risk subset.

Tier 2 (High):
- Standard security questionnaire (lighter version of SIG or a scoped custom form).
- Request SOC 2 or equivalent if available; if not, request a security overview/whitepaper and pen test summary.
- DPA if any personal data involved; standard contract security clauses.
- Reassessment every 12-18 months.
- Business owner attestation of criticality/data classification annually.

Tier 3 (Moderate):
- Lightweight questionnaire (10-15 questions) or self-attestation.
- Verify basic hygiene: MFA, encryption in transit, incident notification clause in contract.
- Reassessment every 2 years or upon contract renewal.

Tier 4 (Low):
- Minimal or no formal diligence beyond standard procurement/legal terms.
- Confirm no data access via a quick intake questionnaire (2-3 questions) to make sure they haven't been mis-tiered.
- No recurring reassessment required unless usage changes.

Step 4: Ongoing governance
- Re-tier vendors whenever scope of data access or integration changes (this is often missed - a vendor that starts as Tier 3 and later gets API access to your CRM should be re-tiered).
- Maintain a central vendor risk register with tier, last assessment date, outstanding issues, and contract renewal dates.
- Set a cadence: Tier 1 annual, Tier 2 every 12-18 months, Tier 3 every 24 months, Tier 4 opportunistic.
- Assign a risk owner (procurement, security, or a dedicated TPRM function) responsible for tracking overdue reassessments and escalating open findings.
- Build in an offboarding checklist for all tiers (access revocation, data return/destruction) since termination risk is often overlooked in TPRM programs.

Given 200 vendors, I'd expect roughly 10-20 in Tier 1, 30-40 in Tier 2, 70-80 in Tier 3, and the remainder in Tier 4 - but validate this against your actual inventory rather than assuming the distribution. Start the tiering exercise with the highest-spend and highest-data-access vendors first, since those carry the most risk exposure and are usually a small percentage of the total vendor count.
