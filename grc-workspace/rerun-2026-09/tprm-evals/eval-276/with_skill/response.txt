This is a Tier 1 vendor by definition — payroll touches PII (SSNs/bank details) for every employee and the business cannot run payroll without it — so evaluate it with full SOC 2 Type II rigor, not a light-touch skim. Walking the report in AICPA order:

**1. Opinion and period.** Unqualified is the right starting point: it means the auditor found the controls suitably designed AND operating effectively across the whole period, not just designed. The period, October 2024–September 2025, is a full 12 months, which is the strong end of typical Type II coverage (6–12 months) — good, this isn't a short-window report gaming an easy stretch.

**2. The coverage gap — this is your biggest issue.** It is now September 2026, a full year after the report period ended. Under the skill's guidance, a bridge (gap) letter is reasonable for coverage up to roughly 3 months past period-end; a report this stale — 12 months — with no successor report in hand is flagged as a red flag, not a minor administrative gap. Request immediately: (a) the successor SOC 2 Type II report covering October 2025 onward if issued, or (b) if not yet issued, a bridge letter from the vendor's MANAGEMENT (never the auditor — auditors don't attest to untested periods) affirming no material adverse changes to the control environment since period end, and a firm date for the next report. Do not accept the 2024–2025 report alone as current assurance for a live payroll relationship in Sept 2026.

**3. Subservice organization — AWS carve-out.** Carve-out is the most common method and is fine in principle, but it means AWS's controls are explicitly excluded from the vendor's testing. The report should list CSOCs (Complementary Subservice Organization Controls) the vendor assumes operate at AWS — physical security, environmental controls, host-level patching, etc. Confirm: (a) the CSOC list is present and specific, not boilerplate; (b) the vendor can show you how it monitors AWS's own assurance (AWS's SOC 2/ISO 27001 reports, which you should also request or at least confirm the vendor reviews); this monitoring obligation exists under either carve-out or inclusive method per the AICPA guide.

**4. The three access management exceptions.** Don't stop at the count — triage each one: which specific criterion/control failed, is it a design failure (systemic, more serious) or an isolated operating miss (e.g., a small sample of terminated-user deprovisioning tickets missed an SLA), and does it touch functionality you actually use (e.g., if the exceptions relate to internal admin console access controls that gate who can see payroll data, that's directly relevant). Three exceptions in one control area is below the "red flag" threshold in this methodology (>5 exceptions in access management triggers automatic red-flag treatment), but access management exceptions for a payroll vendor deserve extra scrutiny given the sensitivity of the data — request the vendor's remediation plan and target closure date from Section 5 (Other Information/management responses), and check whether remediation is complete by now given a year has passed.

**5. CUECs.** Extract the Complementary User Entity Controls section into an internal ownership checklist — commonly things like "customer is responsible for provisioning/deprovisioning its own users," enabling MFA on your side, and configuring data retention settings. An unowned CUEC on your side is effectively your own uncontrolled exposure, regardless of how clean the vendor's report is.

**6. Scope check.** Confirm the trust services categories in scope (Security is baseline; confirm whether Availability is included, which matters more for a payroll processing SLA than Confidentiality alone) and that the system description explicitly covers the payroll processing service and infrastructure you actually consume, not just a broader platform.

**Verdict:** Accept-with-conditions, not a clean accept — contingent on (1) a current bridge letter or successor report within 30 days, (2) written remediation status on the three access-management exceptions, and (3) confirmation of AWS's own current assurance status. Also confirm the vendor holds a signed HIPAA BAA or data-processing terms if any benefits/health data flows through payroll adjacent systems, and log this vendor's cadence as annual full reassessment given its Tier 1 criticality.
