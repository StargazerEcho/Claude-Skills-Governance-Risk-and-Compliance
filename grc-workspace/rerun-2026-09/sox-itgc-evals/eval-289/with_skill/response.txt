**How auditors will actually test this section, end to end:**
1. **Population completeness first** — before sampling, auditors (and you, pre-audit) will confirm the change ticket log is the complete population, typically via BC-CM-05's reconciliation against the system's own deployment/config log. A sample drawn from an incomplete population is worthless, and this is consistently one of PCAOB's most-cited findings.
2. **Sample and trace** — using the 25-item convention for a frequently-changing custom system (agree the exact number with your auditor; it's convention, not rule), trace each sampled change from ticket creation through approval, testing evidence, and migration date, confirming approval and testing precede migration (BC-CM-01/02).
3. **SoD test** — full-population comparison of developer access to deploy-tool access (BC-CM-03), not a sample, since it's a design/configuration fact rather than a per-transaction test.
4. **Emergency changes tested separately** — typically full population given lower volume, checking both the at-the-time authorization and the after-the-fact review (BC-CM-04).
5. **Exceptions drive follow-up**, not automatic failure — any exception found (e.g., a change approved after migration) gets root-caused; isolated timing issues with strong testing evidence otherwise may still support operating effectiveness, but a pattern, or one exception combined with a failed SoD test, escalates to a deficiency evaluation.

Note the sample sizes and cadences above are audit convention (daily/frequent≈25, quarterly≈2), not an SEC or PCAOB rule — confirm the exact numbers with your external auditor before finalizing the test plan, and remember this ITGC section only matters insofar as it supports the automated revenue-calculation and invoicing controls the billing engine performs — if a change-management gap is found, evaluate its severity through those downstream revenue controls, not the ITGC failure in isolation.
