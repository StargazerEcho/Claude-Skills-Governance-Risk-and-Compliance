Two separate questions are bundled here — filer status/timing, and 404(b) attestation — and they don't move together the way people assume, so let's untangle them.

**Filer status.** Under the current thresholds, a company with $450M public float and $180M in annual revenue lands in the "accelerated filer" bucket ($75M–$700M float AND ≥$100M revenue). You're well clear of large accelerated filer (≥$700M float) and above the small reporting company (SRC) cutoff (<$250M float, or <$100M revenue with <$700M float). So on float and revenue alone, you'd be an accelerated filer.

**But you likely qualify as an Emerging Growth Company (EGC).** The JOBS Act (§103) lets a company with under $1.235B in annual revenue retain EGC status for up to five years after IPO (with some earlier-exit triggers: crossing the revenue threshold, issuing >$1B in non-convertible debt over three years, or becoming a large accelerated filer). At $180M revenue you're comfortably under that cap, so unless you've already tripped an exit trigger, you're almost certainly an EGC for at least this fiscal year and probably several more. EGC status is what actually controls your 404(b) obligation — not accelerated-filer status alone.

**When 404(a) bites.** SOX §404(a) — management's own annual ICFR assessment — applies to all public companies, no exemptions for EGCs or newly public companies. But there's a well-established on-ramp: your first annual report (10-K) after the June 2026 IPO can omit the management ICFR assessment entirely. Your first 404(a) conclusion is due in your **second Form 10-K** — for a calendar-year filer that IPO'd mid-2026, that's the 10-K for fiscal year 2027, filed in early 2028. Section 302/906 officer certifications, by contrast, apply starting with your very first periodic report (10-Q or 10-K) — don't confuse those with the ICFR assessment.

**Whether you need the auditor attestation.** 404(b) requires an independent auditor's opinion on ICFR. EGCs are exempt from 404(b) regardless of accelerated-filer status — that exemption is the whole point of the JOBS Act carve-out for newly public companies. So as long as you remain an EGC, you do not need the attestation, even though your float puts you in "accelerated filer" territory. The moment you lose EGC status (five-year cap, revenue crossing $1.235B, or the other triggers above), you'd need to re-test: as an accelerated filer no longer shielded by EGC status, 404(b) would become mandatory starting with the annual report for the fiscal year in which you lose EGC status (subject to the usual transition mechanics — confirm timing with your auditor when that year approaches).

**Practical sequencing for you:** build 404(a)-ready ICFR now even though the public deadline is 18+ months out — walkthroughs, RCMs, and control operation need a track record before your second 10-K, and remediation of anything found needs a clean operating window before that year-end. Don't wait for the EGC clock to run out to start; auditors will want to see at least a year of designed-and-operating controls before your first management assertion, and if you IPO'd with legacy manual controls, program-change and access controls (Domain 1 and 2 ITGCs) are usually the first gaps found.

**One item to flag, not act on:** the SEC issued a proposal on May 19, 2026 that would raise the large-accelerated-filer threshold to $2B float, exempt all non-accelerated filers from 404(b), and add a roughly five-year post-IPO on-ramp. Comments closed July 20, 2026, but this is **not final rule** — treat today's thresholds and EGC mechanics above as the operative law, and revisit before making any multi-year 404(b) commitment.

Bottom line: 404(a) first bites in your second 10-K (FY2027, filed early 2028); 404(b) attestation is not required while you retain EGC status, independent of your accelerated-filer float bracket — but build the ICFR program now so you're not scrambling when either clock runs out.
