Congratulations on passing basic Cyber Essentials - Plus is a meaningfully different exercise, so it's worth resetting expectations now rather than at the audit.

What Plus actually is
Where basic CE is a self-assessment questionnaire reviewed by an assessor, Cyber Essentials Plus is an independent technical verification of the same five control themes (firewalls, secure configuration, user access control, malware protection, patch management) - but tested, not just described. You must hold a valid basic CE certificate (no more than 3 months old at the time Plus testing starts) before you can be assessed for Plus, so timing matters if you've just passed basic.

What happens during the audit
1. Scoping call/planning - the assessor (a certification body, sometimes different from the one that did your basic CE) agrees the scope and a representative sample of devices/servers to test, based on your device population (they use a sampling methodology - roughly the square root of each homogeneous group, with minimums).
2. Vulnerability scanning - internal and external scans of in-scope systems, looking for missing patches, weak configurations, and known vulnerabilities exceeding the 14-day patch window.
3. Authenticated device checks - the assessor logs into a sample of your actual laptops/servers with standard user credentials (not admin) to verify: OS/software patch levels, anti-malware is active and up to date, no unauthorised software, account configuration matches your questionnaire.
4. Malware/phishing simulation - typically involves attempting to download a benign test file (EICAR test file) via email and web browser, and sometimes running a script that mimics ransomware behaviour, to confirm malware protection and browser/email controls actually block or flag it.
5. Account separation and privilege testing - confirming standard users can't perform admin actions, and admin accounts are properly separated from day-to-day use.
6. Remote/cloud considerations - if you're cloud-heavy (M365/AWS), the assessor will test cloud service configuration too (MFA enforcement, admin account controls), often via a combination of remote device testing (agent-based or remote desktop) and cloud console review, since Plus moved to accommodate remote/hybrid workforces.

Where organisations commonly trip up
- Patch drift between passing basic CE and the Plus audit - a gap of even a few weeks can let critical patches slip past the 14-day window, and Plus actually checks this rather than trusting a questionnaire answer.
- Local admin rights left on standard user laptops "for convenience" (e.g., developers, IT staff) - Plus testing directly probes for this.
- Anti-malware not actually running/updated on every sampled device, even if it's installed - definitions out of date, real-time protection disabled, or exclusions configured too broadly.
- Unmanaged or forgotten devices - a laptop that wasn't in the asset inventory used for basic CE surfaces during sampling and fails.
- BYOD/personal devices not meeting the passcode, patching, or configuration bar once actually tested rather than self-reported.
- Legacy or shadow IT systems (that old test server, a forgotten VM, a router with default credentials) that weren't declared in the original scope.
- Email/web filtering not actually blocking the EICAR test file or the safe test scripts used to simulate malicious behaviour - often because a security tool is misconfigured rather than absent.
- Firewalls with looser rules in practice than what was described - e.g., temporary rules opened for a project and never closed.
- Account list mismatches - leavers whose accounts weren't disabled, or shared/generic accounts that don't map to a named individual.

How to prepare well
- Re-run your own internal check of patch levels and anti-malware status across the whole device fleet, not just the sample you expect to be tested - the assessor picks the sample, not you.
- Confirm no standard users have local admin rights, and clean up any leftover admin credentials from IT troubleshooting.
- Test your own email/web security controls against the EICAR test file before the audit.
- Refresh your asset inventory to make sure it's complete and accurate, including any devices added since basic CE.
- Treat Plus prep as an operational hygiene exercise, not a paperwork exercise - most Plus failures come from real technical gaps that basic CE's self-assessment format didn't surface, not from documentation problems.

Typical timeline from booking to certificate is a few weeks, and many organisations schedule Plus within the 3-month validity window of their basic certificate specifically so both stay aligned for the next annual renewal cycle.
