This is a very common profile for a modern small business, and the good news is the scoping rules for exactly this setup (BYOD, fully remote, cloud-only infrastructure) are well defined under the current Cyber Essentials question set (Danzell / Requirements for IT Infrastructure v3.3, mandatory for assessment accounts created from 27 April 2026). Here's how it breaks down.

**Microsoft 365 and AWS: both fully in scope, no exceptions.** Under Danzell, cloud services are formally defined and cannot be excluded from scope if they hold organisational data or deliver organisational services — and both M365 and AWS clearly do. Apply the shared-responsibility model to know which controls are whose:
- **M365 (SaaS)**: Microsoft owns most of the underlying technical controls (patching the service, physical security, etc.), but you always own user access control — meaning account provisioning/deprovisioning, unique credentials, and critically, **MFA enabled on every account that logs into it**. Missing MFA on a cloud service that supports it is an automatic-fail condition under Danzell, so this is not optional or "best practice" — it's a pass/fail line item.
- **AWS (IaaS)**: you own the lion's day of the controls here — secure configuration of your instances, patching guest OSes and applications, access control into the AWS console and workloads, and malware protection on anything that looks like a server/device. AWS owns the physical infrastructure and hypervisor. Same MFA rule applies to the AWS root and IAM accounts.
- For both, don't just assume the provider "handles it" — where you're relying on Microsoft or AWS for a control, confirm it's a contractual commitment (their trust/compliance documentation), not just an assumption.

**Personal phones for email (BYOD): in scope.** The scoping decision tree is simple: if a device accesses organisational data or services — and reading company email clearly qualifies — it's in scope, regardless of who owns it. The only BYOD carve-out is a device used solely for native phone calls/SMS or purely as an MFA authenticator app, which doesn't apply here since staff are reading email on them. Practically, this means each personal phone used for email needs to meet the secure configuration and malware/unlock requirements — device lock (PIN/biometric/password), and typically some form of mobile device management or configuration profile is the practical way to demonstrate and enforce this across a BYOD fleet, though the standard itself just requires the outcome, not a specific tool.

**Everyone working from home: also in scope, but the boundary shifts.** Home and remote working devices — both company-issued and BYOD — are in scope wherever they're used (home, café, train). The important nuance: your staff's home ISP routers are explicitly OUT of scope — you don't need to assess anyone's home broadband router. Instead, the enforced boundary control becomes the software firewall running on each device itself. If you supply staff with your own routers for home use, those would be in scope. And if you route traffic through a corporate or cloud VPN, the boundary shifts again — to that corporate/virtual firewall, which then needs to meet the firewall theme's requirements.

**What is out of scope here:** effectively nothing in your stack, based on what you've described — you don't have any legacy on-prem servers, no dev/test network mentioned, and no third-party contractor or student devices. Your scope statement will be short: whole organisation, all staff (including BYOD phones used for email), M365 tenant, AWS account(s), home working arrangements, no exclusions.

**One scoping mistake to specifically avoid:** teams in your situation sometimes assume "we're all cloud, so there's nothing to certify" or try to exclude BYOD phones because "it's just email." Both are explicitly called out as common mistakes that lead to fails or invalid scope statements — cloud services can never be scoped out under Danzell, and BYOD used for organisational email is squarely in.

**Bottom line for your gap assessment:** your two biggest workstreams will be (1) confirming and enforcing MFA across every M365 and AWS account — this is your automatic-fail risk — and (2) making sure every BYOD phone and home-working device has basic secure configuration (screen lock, no jailbreak/root, auto-updates) and malware protection in place. Everything else flows from a whole-organisation, no-exclusions scope statement.
