Caveat up front: my knowledge of FedRAMP 20x is grounded in its early framing (roughly through late 2025), and I do not have verified, up-to-the-day visibility into "where the pipelines stand as of mid-September 2026." Treat the status portion of this answer as directional context to sanity-check against the current FedRAMP.gov 20x page, PMO announcements, and any direct conversations with the FedRAMP PMO or GSA — not as a confirmed status report.

What FedRAMP 20x is, conceptually: It's the FedRAMP PMO's initiative to fundamentally modernize how cloud services get authorized, moving away from the traditional multi-hundred-page SSP/SAP/SAR/POA&M document cycle with lengthy 3PAO assessments and toward a faster, more automated, continuously validated model. The stated goals include: dramatically reducing time-to-authorization (from many months to weeks), relying on machine-readable evidence (OSCAL, automated key security indicators/KSIs) rather than narrative documents, and enabling continuous validation rather than periodic point-in-time assessment.

On "certification classes" — FedRAMP 20x has been organized around a small number of pilot phases/cohorts rather than a fixed, published taxonomy of permanent "classes" in the way Low/Moderate/High impact levels are a fixed taxonomy under Rev5. As of my knowledge, the structure has looked roughly like this:
- Phase One (Low Impact SaaS pilot): The PMO ran an initial pilot focused on Low-impact SaaS offerings, using a streamlined set of Key Security Indicators (KSIs) instead of the full control catalog narrative, with a small cohort of participating CSPs providing real-world feedback.
- Phase Two / Moderate expansion: The intent was to extend the 20x approach to Moderate impact SaaS and eventually broader IaaS/PaaS offerings, again through cohort-based pilots rather than a general open enrollment, with lessons from Phase One feeding into the KSI catalog and automation tooling.
- Ongoing refinement: KSIs, automated validation ("continuous assessment"), and the underlying technical architecture (APIs for evidence submission, OSCAL-native artifacts) were expected to mature through iterative pilot rounds, with the PMO explicitly stating this was experimental and subject to change based on pilot results, rather than a finished, generally available program.

Because this was explicitly a rolling pilot program rather than a fixed set of "classes" with permanent definitions, I would not assume any specific class names or numbers I might guess at (e.g., "Class A/B/C") are accurate without checking the current FedRAMP 20x page — the PMO has used evolving terminology (KSIs, phases, cohorts) rather than a stable classification scheme, and by September 2026 this may have been reorganized, renamed, or formalized into something closer to an official framework alongside or replacing Rev5.

What I'd recommend to assess "where the pipelines stand" today:
1. Check FedRAMP.gov directly for the 20x program page, which typically lists active pilot cohorts, participating CSPs, and phase status.
2. Look for PMO blog posts or GSA press releases from 2026 summarizing pilot results — these usually disclose how many CSPs completed a given phase, common KSI validation gaps found, and next-phase timing.
3. Ask your 3PAO — 3PAOs participating in 20x pilots are a good source of ground-truth on how mature the automated assessment tooling actually is versus how it's being marketed.
4. Watch for signals about whether 20x is on track to formally supersede parts of Rev5 for specific impact levels/service models, or whether it remains a parallel, optional track for CSPs willing to be early adopters.
5. If you're making a go/no-go decision on participating, ask GSA directly about cohort application windows, since pilot participation has historically been limited and by invitation/application rather than open enrollment.

Bottom line: FedRAMP 20x is a genuine, PMO-driven modernization effort built on KSIs, automation, and phased/cohort-based pilots rather than a settled certification taxonomy — but the specific "classes" and pipeline status as of mid-September 2026 need to be verified against current FedRAMP PMO sources, since this program was explicitly evolving in real time even as of my training data.
