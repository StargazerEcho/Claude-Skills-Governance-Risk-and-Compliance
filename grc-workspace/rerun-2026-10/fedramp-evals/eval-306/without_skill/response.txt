Short answer: I can't point you to an official FedRAMP calendar entry for "September 30, 2026" with full confidence — dates like this move as PMO guidance evolves, and you should verify against the current FedRAMP.gov roadmap and any direct communication from your agency AO and the FedRAMP PMO before treating it as a hard deadline. That said, here is the context a GRC consultant would bring to this question and how to plan around it regardless of the exact date.

Background: FedRAMP has been pushing, for several years, toward machine-readable authorization packages built on OSCAL (Open Security Controls Assessment Language) rather than the traditional Word/Excel SSP, SAP, SAR, and POA&M templates. The PMO has repeatedly signaled that Word/Excel-based submissions are a transitional format and that OSCAL-native (JSON/XML/YAML) packages are the long-term requirement, first for new authorizations and eventually across the board, including for agencies operating the FedRAMP Rev5 baseline. Dates announced for "final" cutovers to OSCAL-only submission have shifted more than once as the PMO calibrated for 3PAO and CSP tooling readiness, so if you've seen September 30, 2026 cited, it's most likely tied to one of these transition milestones (e.g., a deadline for new packages, or for existing packages to convert at their next significant change request or annual assessment).

What this likely means for you if the date is real and applies to your effort:
1. New submissions after that date may be required to be in OSCAL format, meaning your reviewing agency and/or the FedRAMP PMO (if you're going through JAB or seeking a P-ATO) may reject or delay processing of a purely Word/Excel package.
2. If you are mid-process — meaning you already have documents in flight with your agency reviewer — you should immediately ask your agency ISSO/AO point of contact and your 3PAO whether your existing package is grandfathered, whether you need to convert before final authorization, or whether the deadline only affects new starts.
3. Even if there's a grace period, agencies are increasingly asking for OSCAL exports as a courtesy or requirement earlier than mandated, because it feeds their own GRC tooling (e.g., emass, Xacta) more efficiently.

What I'd recommend doing regardless of the exact date:
1. Get your OSCAL conversion plan started now. Converting a mature Word/Excel SSP and control implementation narrative into valid OSCAL is not trivial — it requires mapping every control implementation statement, parameter, and responsibility split (CSP vs. inherited vs. customer) into structured, machine-parseable format. Start this in parallel with your existing authorization work rather than waiting for a deadline to force it.
2. Talk to your 3PAO now about their OSCAL tooling maturity — many 3PAOs are still building capability here, and if yours isn't ready, that becomes your critical path.
3. Ask your agency sponsor directly what format they will accept for the SAR, SSP, and POA&M at each remaining review gate. Some agencies have been pragmatic and accepted Word/Excel through completion of the current authorization cycle, with an OSCAL conversion required only at the next annual assessment or significant change.
4. Watch for official FedRAMP PMO announcements — these come via FedRAMP.gov blog posts, the FedRAMP Rev5 transition guidance, and (more recently) the FedRAMP 20x initiative materials, which are explicitly trying to make machine-readable, API-first submission the default going forward.
5. Budget schedule and cost contingency: OSCAL conversion for a Moderate package with dozens of controls and hundreds of parameters is a real effort — plan for weeks, not days, especially if your control narratives are inconsistent or if inheritance from your IaaS provider isn't cleanly documented.

Bottom line: treat any specific date you've heard as provisional until confirmed directly with the FedRAMP PMO or your agency AO, but don't wait for confirmation to start your OSCAL readiness work — the direction of travel is unambiguous, and being caught flat-footed on format at the finish line of a Moderate authorization would be a costly, avoidable delay.
