With that caveat, here's the substantive guidance a consultant should give you.

Context: FedRAMP's multi-year push toward automation has had two parallel threads. First, the "machine-readable" requirement historically refers to producing your authorization artifacts (SSP, SAP, SAR, POA&M, and the control implementation summary/CIS) in OSCAL format instead of, or in addition to, Word and Excel. Second, more recently, FedRAMP 20x has introduced a broader push toward continuous, API-driven validation of security posture rather than periodic point-in-time documentation review — this is a related but distinct initiative from the OSCAL mandate and has its own track record of pilot phases with a limited cohort of CSPs before wider rollout.

For an existing, already-authorized Rev5 Moderate CSP, the practical trigger points for "when must we convert" tend to be:
1. Annual Assessment (Annual Security Assessment Report cycle) — many agencies and the PMO have signaled this is the natural checkpoint to require OSCAL-formatted deliverables, since you're already refreshing your SAR and POA&M at that time.
2. Significant Change Request (SCR) — if you're filing an SCR for a major architecture change, that's another natural forcing function where reviewers may ask for OSCAL artifacts as part of re-review.
3. Continuous monitoring (ConMon) deliverables — monthly/quarterly ConMon packages (vulnerability scan summaries, POA&M updates) are increasingly expected to be submitted in structured, ingestible formats compatible with agency GRC platforms.
4. A hard PMO-wide cutover date — the PMO has discussed sunset dates for pure Word/Excel acceptance, but historically these dates have been pushed back as tooling and 3PAO readiness lagged. Treat any specific calendar date circulating in your organization as provisional.

What I'd tell a client in your position:
- Don't wait for a hard deadline to become non-negotiable to start. OSCAL conversion of a mature, already-authorized package is a substantial lift: every control implementation statement, responsibility (CSP/inherited/shared/customer), parameter value, and POA&M entry needs to be represented in structured schema-valid form. This is easier to do incrementally (e.g., alongside your next annual assessment) than as a scramble against a deadline.
- Engage your 3PAO early — ask what OSCAL tooling and validation capability they have today, since some 3PAOs are more mature here than others, and their capability constrains your timeline as much as yours does.
- Ask your agency ISSO/AO directly whether they have communicated an internal target date for accepting only machine-readable submissions from you specifically — agency-level implementation of PMO guidance varies, and your actual deadline may be driven more by your sponsoring agency's own modernization timeline than by a PMO-wide date.
- Keep an eye on FedRAMP 20x announcements even though it's a separate track — the PMO's overall strategic direction (fewer point-in-time documents, more continuous machine-validated evidence) will likely eventually fold Rev5 ConMon and reauthorization into a similar automated model, so investments in OSCAL tooling now are not wasted even if timelines shift.
- Budget for a parallel run: many CSPs maintain Word/Excel and OSCAL versions simultaneously during transition to avoid disrupting active agency relationships while building automation muscle.

Bottom line: there is a real, PMO-driven direction toward mandatory machine-readable packages for existing Rev5 CSPs, tied most concretely to your next annual assessment or significant change request cycle rather than a single universal calendar date — confirm the specific date with FedRAMP PMO/your AO, and start the conversion work now rather than treating this as a future problem.
