That said, I can give you a reliable framework for budgeting even before the document drops, based on how SWIFT has run this cycle for years.

How the CSCF release cycle typically works:; SWIFT has followed an annual cadence for the Customer Security Controls Framework since its introduction in 2017. The pattern has generally been:
- New CSCF version published in the summer (historically around July) of the year before the attestation year it governs — e.g., CSCF v2025 was published in 2024, CSCF v2026 in 2025, so if the pattern holds, CSCF v2027 would be expected to publish around mid-2026.
- The attestation window for each version then opens July 1 and closes December 31 of the named year (so v2026 attestations are due by 31 December 2026, and v2027 attestations would be due by 31 December 2027).
- Alongside the new CSCF, SWIFT typically issues a "Summary of Changes" bulletin, updated Independent Assessment Framework (IAF) test procedures, and updated architecture-type guidance.

Budget planning approach — since you're planning ahead of the actual publication:

1. Assume a mid-2026 publication date as your working planning assumption, but build in a contingency of a few months either direction; SWIFT sometimes shifts by a quarter depending on community feedback cycles or major security events driving accelerated control changes.
2. Budget for continuity, not just novelty. Historically, CSCF changes have been incremental — a handful of controls promoted from advisory to mandatory, some clarifications, occasional new controls addressing an emerging threat (e.g., ransomware, cloud-hosted SWIFT interfaces, API-based connectivity). Budget primarily for: (a) gap-analysis effort by your compliance/security team or external advisor, (b) any net-new control remediation (could range from a few thousand dollars for a policy update to significant infrastructure spend if something like network segmentation or MFA scope expands), and (c) the independent assessment/audit fee itself.
3. Independent assessment costs. If you use external assessors (increasingly common/required depending on your architecture type and whether you self-attest or need independent assessment under SWIFT's community-driven assurance requirements), budget for their engagement based on prior-year fees plus inflation, and confirm with them whether they anticipate scope changes.
4. Internal resource time. Even with no major control changes, expect your security/compliance team to need 4-8 weeks of effort for evidence refresh, control walkthroughs, and attestation submission via the KYC-SA (Know Your Customer Security Attestation) portal or the SWIFT attestation application.
5. Training and awareness refresh. If new/changed controls affect staff procedures (e.g., changes to privileged access workflows), budget for a training refresh cycle.
6. Tooling. If historical trends continue toward more automated evidence and API-based verification, consider budgeting for tooling that supports continuous compliance monitoring rather than a once-a-year scramble.

Bottom line: don't commit budget figures based on an assumed CSCF v2027 content until SWIFT actually publishes it. Set a placeholder budget line based on last year's actual spend plus a 10-15% contingency for new mandatory controls, and revisit once the KB publishes the real document — typically you'll have 5-6 months of runway between publication and the 31 December due date, which is enough time to right-size your budget once you see the actual deltas.
