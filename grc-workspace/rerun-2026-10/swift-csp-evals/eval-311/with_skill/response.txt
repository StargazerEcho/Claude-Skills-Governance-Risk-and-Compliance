For your CSCF v2026 attestation, here's the current picture:

**Control count:** CSCF v2026 has 32 total controls — 26 Mandatory and 6 Advisory — across the three security objectives (Secure Your Environment, Know and Limit Access, Detect and Respond). This is one more mandatory control than v2025 had.

**What actually changed from v2025:** The single most significant change is that **Control 2.4 — Back-Office Data Flow Security** was promoted from Advisory to Mandatory. If your architecture previously treated 2.4 as optional and skipped it, you now need to close that gap before this year's attestation window closes.

What Control 2.4 requires in practice:
- Encryption (TLS 1.2+ with certificate-based mutual authentication) on all data flows between the SWIFT secure zone and back-office systems (core banking, trading platforms, payment hubs)
- Authentication of any back-office system connecting into the SWIFT zone
- Network segmentation separating the SWIFT zone from the general back-office VLAN
- Controls preventing unauthorized data exfiltration via back-office channels

The most common gaps organizations find when they move 2.4 from advisory to mandatory: unencrypted messaging between Alliance Access/Gateway and the back-office payment hub, no mutual TLS on those connections, and back-office systems sharing a network segment with the SWIFT zone rather than being isolated behind dedicated firewall rules.

Recommended remediation sequence:
1. Map every data flow between the SWIFT secure zone and back-office systems — you cannot secure what isn't inventoried.
2. Implement TLS 1.2+ with mutual authentication on all identified connections.
3. Route back-office connections through a dedicated interface inside the secure zone rather than a shared gateway.
4. Restrict back-office access with firewall rules scoped to named IPs/ports only.
5. Ensure back-office connection events land in your SIEM alongside your other SWIFT log sources (this ties into Control 6.4).

One important scoping nuance: full back-office coverage is being phased in. The mandatory scope for 2.4 in v2026 covers encryption between the general IT environment and the SWIFT secure infrastructure (including bridging-server flows), but legacy direct back-office connections and the "last hop" from a bridging server to back-office systems are not yet mandatory — SWIFT has signaled that tightening for v2028. Don't over-scope your remediation budget based on requirements that haven't landed yet, but don't assume the phase-in means you can defer the core 2.4 work either.

For your attestation itself, remember the mechanics haven't fundamentally changed: you'll attest via the KYC-SA portal, each mandatory control gets rated Implemented / Partially Implemented / Not Implemented, and — as has been the case since community-standard assessment became the norm — your attestation needs to be backed by an independent assessment (internal audit with sufficient independence, an external firm with SWIFT CSP competency, or a SWIFT-certified assessor).
