That said, here is what I can tell you from general knowledge of how the CSCF framework has evolved, which should help you frame the conversation with your assessor even while you confirm exact numbers:

Structure of the framework: CSCF controls are organized into three objectives — (1) Restrict Internet Access & Protect Critical Systems from General IT Environment, (2) Reduce Attack Surface & Vulnerabilities, and (3) Physically Secure the Environment — further broken into principles and individual controls. Each control is tagged as either "mandatory" or "advisory." Mandatory controls form the baseline that must be attested; advisory controls are recommended best practice and are increasingly being converted to mandatory status over successive CSCF cycles as SWIFT raises the security bar community-wide.

Historical trend: Over the last several CSCF cycles (roughly v2020 through v2025), the mandatory control count has generally hovered in the low-to-mid 20s at the "high level control" grouping, but the count of granular sub-controls that examiners actually test against is higher (often cited in the 30s when counting architecture-specific variations). Each year SWIFT has typically done one of three things: (a) promoted a handful of previously advisory controls to mandatory, (b) clarified or split existing controls to sharpen scope (e.g., around multi-factor authentication, privileged account management, or vulnerability scanning cadence), and (c) adjusted architecture type definitions (A1, A2, A3, A4, B) that determine which controls apply to your specific SWIFT deployment.

Recent thematic changes across versions have generally focused on: strengthening operator/administrator session security, tightening requirements around backup and recovery integrity (ransomware resilience), expanding multi-factor authentication coverage to more interfaces, more rigorous vulnerability and patch management timelines, and clearer expectations for outsourcing/service-provider agreements when a third party operates part of your SWIFT infrastructure.
