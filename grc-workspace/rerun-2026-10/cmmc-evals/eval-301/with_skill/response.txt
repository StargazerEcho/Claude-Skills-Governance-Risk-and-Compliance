As of September 14, 2026, no CMMC Reform Task Force report has been published. Here is the accurate state of play and what it means for your program.

**Timeline recap.** On July 13, 2026, DoD suspended CMMC Phase 2 — specifically the C3PAO third-party assessment requirements that were due to appear in new contract solicitations starting November 10, 2026 — via two policy memoranda (from the DoW CIO and USD(A&S)). That suspension placed pending CMMC Phase 2 milestones in abeyance and stood up a 60-day CMMC Reform Task Force review. The 60-day clock from July 13 expired in mid-September, and today's status is that the report has not been publicly released. Even internal delivery to the DoW CIO is unconfirmed — as of September 9 the CIO indicated the team was still working through more than 1,100 RFI responses. Public release is expected late September to early October, but that timing is unconfirmed and could slip further.

**What has NOT changed.** This is the most important message for your organization: the suspension only touched Phase 2 (C3PAO third-party certification and DIBCAC-led Level 3 assessments). It did not touch the underlying law. 32 CFR Part 170 remains in effect, and DFARS 252.204-7012 (safeguarding CUI + 72-hour DIBNET incident reporting), 7019 (self-assessment), 7020 (SPRS posting), and 7021 (CMMC flow-down) all remain active obligations. Phase 1 self-assessments, SPRS score submissions, and annual senior-official affirmations continue unchanged. During the interim period, requiring activities may only designate Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) in new contract language — not Level 2 (C3PAO) or Level 3 (DIBCAC) — and C3PAO/DIBCAC requirements are being stripped from solicitations and existing contracts at the next option or modification point.

**What the task force is reportedly weighing (signal, not policy).** Public CIO statements — not the report itself, which doesn't exist yet — have suggested dissatisfaction with point-in-time assessments in favor of something closer to continuous-monitoring-style assurance, concern that OT/manufacturing resilience is underrepresented in the current practice set, and interest in reforming CUI-marking discipline across the DIB. Treat these as directional hints from officials, not commitments. Nothing changes in your compliance obligations until DoD issues one of three things: a class deviation, a DFARS rule change, or an amendment to 32 CFR Part 170 itself. A task force report, when it lands, is advisory input to that process — not a self-executing change to your requirements.

**What you should do about it — hold your remediation course.** Concretely:

1. **Do not pause or slow-walk your NIST SP 800-171 remediation work.** Your DFARS 7012/7019/7020 obligations are unaffected, and if you're mid-remediation for an eventual C3PAO assessment, stopping now just re-creates the same gap later when Level 2 (C3PAO) requirements return to new contracts — which the reform outcome may do on short notice.
2. **Keep current self-assessment and SPRS submissions on schedule**, and keep annual senior-official affirmations current; a lapsed affirmation carries False Claims Act exposure independent of the Phase 2 suspension.
3. **Watch for the report's actual publication** — re-verify this status after any DoD announcement, and specifically look for whether it triggers a class deviation or NPRM, since that's the mechanism that would actually change your obligations.
4. **If you're a subcontractor being flowed CMMC requirements**, confirm with your prime whether their contract language has already been modified to Level 1/2 (Self) — if not, ask, since primes are required to strip C3PAO/DIBCAC language at the next option/mod.

In short: nothing is settled, nothing in your underlying compliance obligation has moved, and the safest posture is to keep executing your 800-171 program as if Phase 2 could return at any time — because it may.
