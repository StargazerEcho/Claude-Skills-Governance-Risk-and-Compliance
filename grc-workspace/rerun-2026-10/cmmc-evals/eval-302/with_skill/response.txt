No. Deprioritizing NIST SP 800-171 work this year would be a mistake, and here is why in concrete regulatory terms.

**The suspension only touches Phase 2 — not your underlying obligations.** On July 13, 2026, DoD suspended CMMC Phase 2, meaning the C3PAO third-party assessment requirement (Level 2 (C3PAO)) and DIBCAC-led Level 3 assessments due to appear in new solicitations from November 10, 2026 are on hold pending a CMMC Reform Task Force review. As of today, September 14, 2026, no task force report has been published — the 60-day review window closed in mid-September with nothing public yet, and even internal delivery to the DoW CIO was unconfirmed as of September 9. Expected release is late September to early October, unconfirmed.

Critically, that suspension did **not** touch 32 CFR Part 170, and it did not touch DFARS 252.204-7012 (safeguarding CUI, 72-hour DIBNET incident reporting), 7019 (NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment), 7020 (SPRS score submission), or 7021 (CMMC flow-down). All of those remain in force. If your contract requires you to protect CUI, your obligation to implement the 110 NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 practices — and to self-assess against them — has not moved at all. What changed is which *assessment track* applies: contracting activities can currently only designate Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self), not Level 2 (C3PAO) or Level 3 (DIBCAC). Self-assessment did not go away; if anything, it's now the *only* track being written into new contract language.

**Practical consequences of deprioritizing:**
- Your annual SPRS submission and senior-official affirmation obligations continue unchanged. A stale or inaccurate affirmation carries False Claims Act exposure regardless of what the task force ultimately recommends.
- DoD retains audit rights over self-assessments, and contracting officers can still act on SPRS scores today.
- The task force's public direction signals (from CIO statements, not the unreleased report) lean toward *more* assurance over time — continuous-monitoring-style oversight, tighter CUI-marking discipline, and attention to OT/manufacturing resilience — not a loosening of the underlying control set. Slowing remediation now bets against the likely direction of travel.
- If Phase 2 resumes on the same or a shortened timeline once the task force reports out, contractors who paused remediation will face the same 9–18 month first-timer runway to C3PAO readiness, compressed against whatever new deadline is set.
- Nothing in the suspension is self-executing policy change. Your obligations only shift via a class deviation, a DFARS rule amendment, or a 32 CFR Part 170 amendment — none of which has happened.

**What to actually do this year:**
1. Continue full-scope NIST SP 800-171 gap remediation as if C3PAO assessment could return to your contract at any point — because the interim rule explicitly signals it might, on short notice, once the review concludes.
2. Keep your SSP current, keep POA&M items moving (with root-cause remediation, not just symptom patches), and keep your SPRS score accurate and affirmed annually.
3. Prioritize the highest-impact, hardest-to-remediate practices now — MFA (IA.L2-3.5.3), FIPS-validated cryptography (SC.L2-3.13.8/3.13.11), CUI flow control (AC.L2-3.1.3), and audit logging (AU.L2-3.3.1) — since these are both the largest SPRS point deductions and the practices that can never appear in a POA&M at certification.
4. If you're a subcontractor, confirm with your prime whether their DFARS 7021 flow-down language has been updated to Level 1/2 (Self); that doesn't change what you owe on 800-171 itself.

Bottom line: the suspension is a pause on *how* you'll be assessed, not *what* you must implement. Treat this as a compliance-maintenance year, not a deprioritization year, and re-verify this guidance once the task force report actually publishes.
