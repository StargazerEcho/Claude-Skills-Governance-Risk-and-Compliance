Now, here is the structural knowledge that matters for interpreting whatever actually happened, because these distinctions are exactly where people get confused about "what changed":

1. CMMC has multiple legally distinct components, and an action can hit one without touching the others:
 - The 32 CFR Part 170 rule - establishes the CMMC program itself (levels, assessment types, scoring).
 - The DFARS 252.204-7021 clause rule - actually requires CMMC certification as a condition of contract award; this is what needs to be in individual contracts/solicitations for CMMC to be enforceable on a given procurement.
 - The underlying DFARS 252.204-7012 clause - requires NIST SP 800-171 implementation and SPRS self-assessment reporting; this predates CMMC and is independent of it.
 - The C3PAO ecosystem and CMMC Accreditation Body (Cyber AB) - the assessor infrastructure that conducts third-party assessments.

2. A "suspension" could mean, among other things:
 - A pause in adding the 7021 clause to new contract solicitations (meaning no new contracts require CMMC certification during the pause), while the underlying 800-171 obligations in existing 7012 clauses remain fully in force.
 - A pause in the phased rollout schedule (delaying when Level 2/3 certification requirements become mandatory across acquisition categories) without rescinding the rule itself.
 - A halt on new C3PAO assessments or accreditation actions pending some review, without touching contractor obligations at all.
 - A withdrawal or reopening for comment of part of the rule, which would need a new Federal Register notice to resolve.
 - It's very unlikely to mean that NIST SP 800-171 compliance obligations under existing DFARS 7012 clauses are void - those exist independently in your contracts and would require separate contract modifications or a change to 7012 itself to remove.

3. What a suspension almost never does:
 - It does not retroactively change your contractual obligations on contracts already awarded with CMMC or NIST 800-171 clauses in them - those clauses stay in force until the contract is modified.
 - It does not eliminate your obligation to protect CUI under whatever security requirements your contract cites.
 - It does not remove your existing SPRS score obligations or the risk of False Claims Act exposure for inaccurate self-assessments.
 - It does not typically apply retroactively to assessments already completed or in progress unless explicitly stated.
