Here is precisely what the July 13, 2026 CMMC suspension did, and — just as important — what it left untouched.

**What it did.** On July 13, 2026, DoD issued two policy memoranda — one from the DoW CIO, one from USD(A&S) — suspending **CMMC Phase 2**. Phase 2 is the rollout stage that introduces third-party C3PAO assessment for Level 2 (Advanced) and government-led DIBCAC assessment for Level 3 (Expert). Specifically, the suspension:
- Halted the requirement for **C3PAO third-party assessments** that were scheduled to start appearing in new contract solicitations as of November 10, 2026.
- Placed all pending CMMC Phase 2 rollout milestones **in abeyance** — meaning paused, not cancelled — while a review takes place.
- Stood up a **60-day CMMC Reform Task Force review**, with a report originally due around September 13, 2026. As of today, September 14, 2026, that report has not been published; even its internal delivery to the DoW CIO is unconfirmed, with the CIO indicating on September 9 that the team was still working through over 1,100 RFI responses. Public release is expected late September to early October (unconfirmed).
- Directed that, during the interim, requiring activities (contracting offices) may only designate **Level 1 (Self)** or **Level 2 (Self)** in contract requirements — not Level 2 (C3PAO) or Level 3 (DIBCAC).
- Triggered removal of **C3PAO/DIBCAC-specific requirements** from existing solicitations and active contracts, but only at the next contract option period or modification — it is not an instantaneous rewrite of every current contract.

**What it did NOT do.**
- It did **not** repeal or suspend **32 CFR Part 170**, the CMMC 2.0 final rule itself. The rule remains law; only the third-party assessment track under it is paused.
- It did **not** touch any of the underlying **DFARS clauses**: 252.204-7012 (safeguarding CUI and the 72-hour DIBNET incident reporting requirement), 252.204-7019 (NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment requirement), 252.204-7020 (SPRS score submission and DoD access to results), or 252.204-7021 (the CMMC flow-down clause itself, which still requires primes to flow requirements to subcontractors). All four clause families remain fully enforceable.
- It did **not** suspend **Phase 1** activity: annual self-assessments, SPRS score submissions, and senior-official annual affirmations all continue exactly as before. Contractors still must submit accurate scores and affirm them; false affirmations still carry **False Claims Act** exposure.
- It did **not** relieve contractors of any obligation to actually implement the **110 NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 practices** if they handle CUI. The practices, the SPRS scoring methodology, and the POA&M rules are all unchanged.
- It did **not** produce a final outcome yet. The Reform Task Force's public statements (dissatisfaction with point-in-time assessment in favor of more continuous-monitoring-style assurance, OT/manufacturing resilience concerns, CUI-marking discipline) are CIO commentary, not policy — the report is **advisory**. Nothing in your compliance posture changes until DoD acts on it through one of three mechanisms: a class deviation, a DFARS rule change, or an amendment to 32 CFR Part 170.
- It did **not** cancel Level 3 as a concept — DIBCAC-led assessment is paused along with Level 2 (C3PAO), not eliminated.

**Net effect for contractors:** the assessment *mechanism* for Level 2/3 (who checks your work and how) is on hold; the *substance* of what you must implement and self-report is fully intact. Contractors should treat this as a pause button on third-party/government-led certification, not a green light to relax NIST SP 800-171 remediation, SPRS accuracy, or DFARS 7012 incident-reporting discipline. Re-verify this analysis once the Task Force report is actually published, since it could recommend restoring Phase 2 on a new timeline, revising it, or something else — none of which is known yet.
