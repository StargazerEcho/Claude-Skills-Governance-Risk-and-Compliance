The Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) entered into force on 10 December 2024 but rolls out on a staggered timeline, and September 2026 happens to sit right at one of the key inflection points. Here's the picture, split into what's live now versus what's still ahead.

Already in force / applies now (as of September 2026):

1. Reporting obligations (Article 14) — applicable since 11 September 2026 (21 months after entry into force). This is the big one that just kicked in and is likely the reason for your question. Manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU must now notify ENISA (which relays to your national CSIRT and market surveillance authorities) of:
   - Actively exploited vulnerabilities: 24-hour early warning, 72-hour follow-up notification, 14-day final report after a fix is available.
   - Severe incidents affecting the product's security: same 24h/72h structure, with a final report within one month.
   You also have a parallel duty to inform affected users without undue delay. This obligation is live today and is not waiting for 2027 — if you have a qualifying vulnerability or incident right now, the clock is running.

2. Conformity assessment body designation (Article 43-ish machinery) — applicable since 11 June 2026 (18 months after entry into force). Member states can now designate/notify conformity assessment bodies ("notified bodies") under the CRA, so the infrastructure for third-party assessment is starting to come online. This matters if your product needs mandatory third-party assessment (Class II important products, or Class I products without full harmonized-standard coverage) — you can begin engaging notified bodies now, and probably should, given expected capacity constraints closer to 2027.

3. General good-practice expectations. Even though the substantive essential requirements (Annex I) and CE-marking machinery aren't legally mandatory until December 2027, market expectations, procurement requirements, and your own risk exposure mean you should already be building: vulnerability-handling processes, SBOM practices, secure-by-design documentation, and incident-response playbooks — because (a) the reporting obligations above only work if you already have the internal muscle to detect and assess incidents/vulnerabilities quickly, and (b) you won't be able to stand up compliant Annex I documentation and processes overnight in late 2027.

Still ahead:

4. Full application — 11 December 2027 (36 months after entry into force). This is when the bulk of the CRA becomes binding: the Annex I essential requirements (secure by design/default, vulnerability handling, secure update mechanisms, etc.), the conformity assessment obligations proper (self-assessment or notified-body routes depending on classification), CE marking, EU Declarations of Conformity, technical documentation requirements, and the due-diligence obligations on importers and distributors (checking manufacturers have done their conformity assessment, CE marking is affixed, documentation exists, etc.). After this date, products with digital elements generally cannot be placed on the EU market without CRA conformity.

5. Transitional treatment of products already on the market. Products with digital elements already placed on the market before 11 December 2027 are generally not required to retroactively meet the full CRA regime unless they undergo a "substantial modification" after that date — but the Article 14 reporting obligations apply regardless of when the product was placed on the market, since reporting obligations are tied to the manufacturer's ongoing awareness of vulnerabilities/incidents, not to a product's original compliance date. That's an important nuance: even a pre-2024 product still being sold or supported today is inside the reporting regime now.

Bottom line for "right now" (September 2026): your live, binding obligation is the Article 14 vulnerability/incident reporting regime — get your CSIRT/ENISA reporting channel, 24/72-hour internal escalation process, and user-notification templates operational immediately if they aren't already. Your December 2027 obligation is the full essential-requirements/CE-marking/conformity-assessment package — treat the next 15 months as your build-out runway, including notified-body engagement if your product needs third-party assessment. I'd recommend confirming your product's Annex III classification (default / Class I / Class II) soon, since that determines how much of the 2027 workload is self-manageable versus dependent on third-party assessment capacity that is likely to tighten as the deadline approaches.
