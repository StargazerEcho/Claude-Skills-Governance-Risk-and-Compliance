A network firewall appliance with a companion management app sits squarely in the CRA's "important products with digital elements" category (Annex III), which carries heavier conformity obligations than the default/general "product with digital elements" tier. Here's how classification and conformity assessment should work through.

Classification. Annex III splits important products into Class I and Class II:
- Class I includes, among other things, firewalls, intrusion detection and prevention systems that are not intended for industrial use, along with things like VPNs, password managers, identity management systems, network management systems, and standalone/embedded browsers.
- Class II is reserved for a narrower set of more critical items — firewalls and intrusion detection/prevention systems intended for industrial use, hypervisors/container runtimes supporting virtualized execution of operating systems, smart meter gateways, HSMs, secure elements/smart cards, and similar.

If your firewall appliance is a general enterprise/network security product (not specifically marketed and designed for industrial/OT environments — e.g., not an industrial control system firewall), it falls into Class I. If it's specifically intended for industrial automation and control environments, it would fall into the stricter Class II bucket. Given your description ("network firewall appliance with a management app," no industrial framing), I'd treat it as Class I unless your actual go-to-market positions it for OT/ICS use — that distinction is worth confirming precisely because it changes your conformity assessment route.

The management app. Under the CRA, "remote data processing solutions" that the manufacturer is responsible for and that are necessary for the product to perform one of its functions are treated as part of the product with digital elements, not as a separate unregulated add-on. If your management app is the mechanism through which the appliance is configured, monitored, or receives policy/updates, it will generally be swept into the same product boundary and same conformity assessment as the appliance itself — so your technical documentation, risk assessment, SBOM, and vulnerability-handling processes need to cover the app (including its backend/cloud components if it's not purely on-device) as well as the hardware/firmware.

Conformity assessment route:
- Class I products may use self-assessment (internal control, "Module A") only if the manufacturer has applied harmonized standards, European cybersecurity certification schemes, or common specifications covering all the applicable essential requirements in Annex I. If you can fully map your product against relevant harmonized standards (once published — many are still being drafted by CEN/CENELEC) and demonstrate full compliance, self-assessment is available.
- If harmonized standards don't exist yet, are only partially applied, or you choose not to rely on them, Class I products must go through third-party conformity assessment — either the EU-type examination plus conformity-to-type module (Module B + C) or the full quality-assurance module (Module H), performed by a notified body.
- Class II products cannot self-assess at all — third-party assessment via notified body is mandatory regardless of standards, given their higher risk profile.
- There's also a small "critical products" list (Annex IV) requiring mandatory European cybersecurity certification once a relevant scheme exists — this is very narrow and unlikely to capture a standard firewall appliance, but worth a quick check against Annex IV since the list can be expanded by delegated act.

Given harmonized standards under the CRA are still being finalized as of 2026, a lot of Class I manufacturers are currently expected to need — or to at least be preparing for — third-party involvement (notified body) rather than pure self-assessment, simply because "full application of harmonized standards" isn't yet demonstrable for most product categories. I'd recommend engaging a notified body early for a gap assessment even if you hope to eventually rely on self-assessment, since notified-body capacity is expected to be a bottleneck as the December 2027 full-application deadline approaches.

Practical next steps: (1) confirm definitively whether your firewall is Class I or Class II based on actual marketed use-case (industrial vs. general); (2) map your management app's architecture (on-device vs. cloud backend) to determine what falls inside the regulated product boundary; (3) start (or continue) your Annex I essential-requirements risk assessment and technical documentation now, since that underlies whichever assessment route you take; (4) if you'll need a notified body, start that engagement now given lead times; and (5) build your SBOM and vulnerability-handling/coordinated-disclosure process, since those are needed regardless of route and also feed your Article 14 incident/vulnerability reporting obligations, which are already in force.
