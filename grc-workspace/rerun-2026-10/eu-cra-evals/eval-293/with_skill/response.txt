## CRA Scope and Classification — Network Firewall Appliance with Management App

**Scope determination:** In scope. A network firewall with a management application is a hardware product with remote data processing (the management app) and at least one network interface — this squarely meets the CRA's definition of a Product with Digital Elements (PDE). None of the standard exclusions (medical devices, aviation, automotive type-approval, military/national security, classified systems) apply to a firewall appliance.

**Product class: it depends on the deployment context, and you need to check both Annex III and Annex IV before settling on a class.**

- **If this is a general/home-use or SMB firewall** without a specific critical-infrastructure or industrial designation, it most likely falls under **Class I (Annex III)**. The Annex III list explicitly includes "web application firewalls" and "network filters," and firewalls generally sit within the network security and monitoring category alongside VPNs, SIEM systems, and network traffic monitoring tools — all Class I. A firewall's core function (controlling and monitoring network traffic, enforcing access boundaries) is exactly the kind of security-relevant function the CRA treats as higher-risk than a generic connected product.

- **If this is specifically an industrial firewall intended for critical infrastructure or OT/ICS environments** (energy, water, transport sectors), it escalates to **Class II (Annex IV)**, which explicitly lists "industrial firewalls, intrusion detection/prevention systems (IDS/IPS) intended for use in critical infrastructure or OT environments." This is a materially different — and stricter — regulatory path, so confirm your target market/use case before finalizing classification. If you sell the same appliance into both enterprise and critical-infrastructure/OT deployments, you may need to treat the OT-targeted SKU or configuration as Class II even if a general enterprise variant would be Class I.

- **The management app** is generally treated as part of the same product (a "remote data processing solution" under the PDE definition) rather than as a separately classified product, so it inherits the same class as the firewall appliance it manages — but it is squarely in-scope for Annex I requirements in its own right (authentication, access control, encrypted management traffic, secure update mechanism, etc.).

**Applicable Annex:** Annex III (Class I) for a general-purpose/enterprise firewall — most likely bucket under "network filters" / "web application firewalls" / network monitoring categories. Annex IV (Class II) if the product is specifically marketed or designated for critical infrastructure/OT use as an industrial firewall.

**Conformity assessment route:**
- **Class I:** You have a choice — **Module A self-assessment** or **Notified Body assessment**. Self-assessment is permitted but is not self-certification without evidence: you must have genuine technical evidence (test reports, risk assessment, documented controls) that Annex I requirements are met, retained for 10 years, and inspectable by market surveillance authorities on request. Many organizations building security-focused products like firewalls opt for voluntary Notified Body review anyway, since it can strengthen customer trust and streamline enterprise/government procurement, but it isn't mandatory.
- **Class II:** **Mandatory Notified Body assessment.** No self-assessment option. Note that notified body designation across member states isn't required until 11 December 2026, so a transitional period applies; expect assessment timelines of 6–18 months for complex products once the process is operational, so plan your market-entry timeline accordingly.

**Key obligations summary (applies regardless of Class I vs II, with Class II adding NB involvement):**
1. Meet all Annex I Part I security properties — secure-by-default configuration, strong authentication/access control on both the firewall and the management app, encrypted management traffic, signed firmware/software updates with signature verification, resilience against DoS (notable for a firewall, which is itself a DoS mitigation tool and must not become a DoS victim/vector itself), and exploit mitigation mechanisms.
2. Stand up the full Annex I Part II vulnerability handling program: CVD/VDP policy with a published contact, SBOM (SPDX/CycloneDX recommended) covering top-level dependencies for both firmware and the management app, CERT/CSIRT information sharing, and free security updates for the support period (minimum 5 years).
3. Prepare Annex VII technical documentation — architecture and data-flow diagrams, network connectivity diagram (interfaces/protocols/ports — especially relevant given this is a firewall), threat model, cryptographic measures, test reports, and vulnerability-handling documentation.
4. Draw up the EU Declaration of Conformity (Annex V), affix CE marking (plus the Notified Body's four-digit ID if Class II or if you opt for NB review as Class I), and retain TD/DoC for 10 years.
5. **Register in the EU database** before market placement — mandatory for both Class I and Class II products (expected operational by December 2027); Default-class products are exempt but yours isn't.
6. Since 11 September 2026, you have live obligations to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA/national CSIRTs via the ENISA Single Reporting Platform, on the 24-hour/72-hour/final-report clock — this applies now, ahead of the full CRA application date of 11 December 2027.

**Recommendation:** Definitively confirm your target deployment (enterprise/home vs. critical-infrastructure/OT) before committing to a conformity route, since it changes whether Notified Body review is optional or mandatory and materially affects your timeline to market. If you sell into both segments, consider whether to certify the whole line to the stricter Class II standard to simplify your compliance program, or maintain separate SKUs/configurations with separate classifications.

This is general compliance information, not legal advice — confirm final classification with an accredited assessor or qualified counsel given the material difference in obligations between Class I and Class II.
