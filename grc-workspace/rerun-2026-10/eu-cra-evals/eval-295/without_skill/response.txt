Onboarding to CRA reporting isn't a single self-service "create an account" flow the way, say, a SaaS vendor portal is — it's built around ENISA's single reporting platform (mandated by Article 16 of the CRA) that channels notifications to national CSIRTs and market surveillance authorities. Since this infrastructure is still relatively new (the underlying reporting obligation only became applicable on 11 September 2026), expect some friction and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction variation as it matures. Here's how to approach it.

Who registers. The obligation to report sits with the manufacturer of the product with digital elements (Article 14). If you're a non-EU manufacturer, your EU-based authorized representative can also be a point of contact, and importers/distributors have secondary obligations to inform manufacturers/authorities if they become aware of a vulnerability or incident, but the primary registrant and reporter is the manufacturer. In practice, the entity or role that should register is whoever owns your product security / PSIRT function — the team responsible for vulnerability handling and incident response — since they'll be the ones filing under time pressure.

How to register/onboard, practically:
1. Identify your "home" CSIRT. Under both NIS2 and the CRA, each manufacturer typically routes through the CSIRT designated as coordinator in the member state where it is established (or, for non-EU manufacturers, the state of their authorized representative). Start by contacting that national CSIRT/single point of contact directly — most member states have a national cybersecurity agency (e.g., a national CERT/CSIRT) with published guidance and intake channels, and many are actively onboarding manufacturers ahead of/around the CRA reporting obligation coming into force.
2. Register with ENISA's reporting platform once access is available to you. ENISA is responsible for building and operating the single reporting platform under Article 16, intended to let manufacturers submit early warnings, notifications, and final reports in a structured way and have that information automatically routed to the relevant CSIRT and market surveillance authority. Because this is a relatively new piece of shared EU infrastructure, availability, exact registration steps (e.g., whether it requires an EU Login/eIDAS-based identity, a company registration process, API access for high-volume reporters, etc.) may still be rolling out unevenly — check ENISA's official CRA guidance pages and your national CSIRT for the current registration procedure rather than assuming a single universal portal experience already exists everywhere.
3. Prepare your internal reporting workflow before you need it. Regardless of platform mechanics, you need: a defined "become aware" trigger, an on-call/escalation path that can produce a 24-hour early warning, a template covering the minimum required fields (nature of vulnerability/incident, affected product(s)/versions, member states affected, exploitation status), and a document trail. Registering your team's contact details and authentication credentials with the CSIRT/platform ahead of an actual incident avoids losing time during the critical first 24 hours.

What if the platform is down when you need it. The CRA's reporting deadlines are not suspended just because ENISA's platform has an outage — the obligation is to notify "without undue delay" and within the stated hour/day windows, so you need a fallback:
- Contact your national CSIRT directly through its published out-of-platform channel (phone, dedicated incident email, or an alternate web form) — national CSIRTs generally maintain 24/7 contact points for exactly this kind of situation, independent of the EU-level platform.
- Document the outage and your attempted submission time/timestamp — this evidence will matter if a market surveillance authority later questions timeliness; regulators generally expect good-faith efforts and reasonable alternative channels to be used, not silence, when the primary system is unavailable.
- Submit the full notification through the platform as soon as it's restored, referencing your earlier direct CSIRT contact so the record is complete and continuous.
- If you have a NIS2 relationship with the same CSIRT (many manufacturers of important products also have obligations or informal relationships under NIS2), you may already have an established direct channel you can use as your default fallback — worth confirming this mapping in advance rather than during an actual incident.

Because the operational and platform-level details here are still being finalized/rolled out at the EU and member-state level, I'd treat this as a live-document problem: assign someone to monitor ENISA's and your national CSIRT's published guidance specifically for CRA reporting-platform mechanics over the next several months, since exact registration steps and outage-fallback procedures are likely to be clarified and possibly changed as the system matures toward the December 2027 full-application deadline.
