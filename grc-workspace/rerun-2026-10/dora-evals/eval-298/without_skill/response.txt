Yes — DORA applies to you, and the fact that your parent is a Singapore bank doesn't exempt the Luxembourg branch.

Why DORA applies:
DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) applies to "financial entities" as defined in Article 2, and that definition is drawn from existing EU sectoral legislation (CRD/CRR for credit institutions, etc.) using the concept of authorization/establishment in the EU. A branch of a third-country (non-EEA) bank that is authorized to operate in an EU member state — here, Luxembourg, via the CSSF — is generally treated as falling within scope for the activities conducted through that branch, because the branch itself is licensed and supervised under EU/Luxembourg banking law (transposing CRD requirements for third-country branches). In practice, this means:
- The Luxembourg branch must comply with DORA's ICT risk management framework requirements (Chapter II) for the activities and systems it operates in/through the branch.
- It must maintain its own (or a clearly documented group-shared but branch-specific) ICT risk management framework, incident classification and reporting processes, digital operational resilience testing, and third-party risk management including its Register of Information entries for ICT contracts relevant to the branch.
- Incident reporting obligations run to the CSSF (Luxembourg's competent authority) for major ICT-related incidents affecting the branch's activities.
- If the branch relies on group-level or Singapore head-office ICT systems and providers, those arrangements must still be captured in the branch's Register of Information and risk assessments — DORA does not accept "the parent handles it" as a substitute for branch-level accountability, though group-wide arrangements and simplified frameworks may be usable for entities meeting certain size/risk criteria (Article 16's simplified ICT risk management framework applies to smaller/less complex entities such as small non-interconnected investment firms — check whether your branch qualifies for any proportionality relief, though large or systemically relevant branches generally will not).

Key nuance on third-country branches: The regulatory treatment of third-country branches under DORA has been a genuinely debated area, because DORA's core definitions are entity-based (credit institutions authorized under CRD) rather than explicitly branch-based, and there have been ongoing clarifications from the European Commission, EBA, and national competent authorities about the precise scope of application to non-EEA bank branches, especially regarding which parts of the ICT risk framework must be maintained locally versus can be inherited from group policies. The CSSF has generally taken the position that branches of third-country credit institutions established in Luxembourg fall under its supervisory remit for DORA purposes for the activities booked through the branch, consistent with its broader approach to third-country branch supervision.
