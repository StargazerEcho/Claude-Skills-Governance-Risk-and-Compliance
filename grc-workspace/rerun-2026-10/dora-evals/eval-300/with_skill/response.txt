DORA's contractual requirements for ICT providers are set out in **Art. 30**, detailed further by **CDR (EU) 2024/1773** (general contractual content) and **CDR (EU) 2025/532** (sub-contracting specifically). The scope and rigor of what's required depends on whether the arrangement supports a **critical or important function (CIF)**.

**Step 1 — Determine which regime applies**

- If the ICT service supports a critical or important function (disruption would materially impair legal compliance, financial performance, or continuity of services), the **full Art. 30(2)(a)–(i)** provisions apply.
- If it does not, a **lighter set of provisions** applies under Art. 30(3): service description, data locations, basic availability/security commitments, incident notification, and exit/termination terms. Do not assume every vendor needs the full checklist — that is a common over-application error.

**Step 2 — For critical/important arrangements, the mandatory Art. 30(2) clauses are:**

| Clause | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **(a) Service description** | Clear, complete description of the ICT services provided |
| **(b) Data locations** | Where services are performed and data stored/processed, plus notification obligations if locations change |
| **(c) Data protection** | Provisions ensuring compliance with applicable data protection law (e.g., GDPR) |
| **(d) Availability/integrity/security** | Service levels, security standards, and the provider's incident response obligations |
| **(e) Audit and access rights** | Full and unrestricted audit rights for your entity, plus access rights for your competent authority and resolution authority — including on-site inspection |
| **(f) Termination rights** | Clear termination conditions, minimum notice periods, and transition-service obligations on the provider |
| **(g) Reporting and monitoring** | The provider's obligation to report ICT incidents to you, plus ongoing performance monitoring |
| **(h) Data portability/migration** | Machine-readable data return on termination, migration assistance, and deletion certification |
| **(i) Sub-contracting** | Conditions for the provider to sub-contract, prior written consent, equivalent provisions flowing down to sub-processors, and your right to audit sub-processors (detailed further in CDR (EU) 2025/532) |

**Step 3 — Watch the audit-rights clause (e) — this is the most commonly failed provision**

Large cloud/SaaS providers often offer only pooled third-party audit reports (SOC 2, ISO 27001) rather than direct, entity-specific audit rights. ESA guidance allows pooled audits to *partially* satisfy your own audit right **only if** you document in writing why a direct audit is impracticable and confirm the pooled outputs are meaningful — with that acceptance approved at an appropriate governance level (e.g., CRO or board). Critically, **the competent authority's and resolution authority's on-site inspection right is non-waivable**: even if you accept pooled audits for your own purposes, the contract must still contain an express, unconditional clause preserving your regulator's right to inspect the provider's premises directly. A clause that routes regulator access through the vendor's own third-party audit programme does **not** satisfy Art. 30(2)(e) — this is a frequent gap in standard cloud contracts.

**Step 4 — Before renewing any CIF contract (Art. 30(4))**

Confirm service levels remain adequate, audit and access rights remain practically exercisable (not just present on paper), your exit strategy is still viable, and no new concentration or substitutability risks have emerged since signing.

**Practical checklist to run against each existing contract:**
- Service description present and current
- Primary and secondary data locations specified, with change-notification clause
- Data protection/GDPR provisions included
- Service levels and security standards defined
- Audit rights for your entity, your competent authority, and resolution authority — each separately confirmed
- Termination-for-cause and termination-for-regulatory-reasons clauses, with minimum notice period
- Incident reporting obligation on the provider
- Data portability, migration assistance, and deletion certification on exit
- Sub-contracting consent and flow-down provisions, with sub-processor audit rights

Any contract missing the audit/access clause, the non-waivable regulator inspection right, or the sub-contracting flow-down provisions should be flagged as a priority remediation item — these are the three gaps regulators most commonly cite.

*General compliance information, not legal advice — have contracts reviewed against CDR (EU) 2024/1773 and CDR (EU) 2025/532 by qualified counsel before relying on this checklist for negotiation.*
